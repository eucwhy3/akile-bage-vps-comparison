## What AkileCloud is, and what it isn't

AkileCloud sells bandwidth and location coverage, and it sells streaming unlock as a standard feature. Nearly every plan ships with the provider's own DNS-based unlock for streaming services and AI tools, which is why people buy it for Netflix regions and ChatGPT access rather than for anything latency-sensitive.

The thing to internalise before ordering: the company does not claim mainland China direct connection for its nodes. That wording comes from AkileCloud itself, and it's a design choice rather than a defect. Hong Kong and Singapore boxes are close to China geographically, but "close" and "optimised return path" are different products. If you're in Shenzhen and want a US box that answers instantly, this isn't that.

Traffic overage is the second thing. Once you burn through the monthly allowance, the port drops to a shared 10Mbps, and you can buy more traffic if you'd rather not live at that speed. For a single HD stream, SSH or API calls, 10Mbps shared is survivable. For pulling a 50GB dataset overnight it is not, and no amount of port speed on the invoice changes that.

There's no standing public promo code. The pricing model is a low base rate plus occasional site-wide specials, so check the site for what's live rather than hunting for a coupon that stopped working last quarter.

## What Bage documents, and why it matters before you pay

BAGE CLOUD LLC sells VPS, cloud servers, dedicated servers, server hosting, IP addresses, network and DDoS protection, per its own privacy policy dated August 1, 2026. Plan-level specifications and prices are not part of the public documentation available here, so this article quotes no Bage price and no Bage configuration. What is documented is the contract, and for the crowd that buys cheap overseas VPS, the contract is where the surprises live.

Its acceptable-use policy prohibits, among other things:

- Sustained heavy consumption of shared network, CPU, memory or disk I/O
- TikTok and other third-party platform automation, bulk activity or evasion tooling that triggers abuse complaints or damages IP/ASN reputation
- Spam and unsolicited bulk email
- Unauthorised scanning, interference or disruption of other systems
- Open proxies, anonymous public proxies, open relays, open recursive DNS resolvers and Tor exit nodes
- Unlawful, infringing or fraudulent content

Abuse notices forwarded to you require a response within 24 hours unless the notice says otherwise. Fail to respond, stop the violation or fix it, and the account can be suspended or terminated with no refund eligibility. Services are unmanaged, which means the operating system, applications, patches, backups and data security are yours to handle. Auto-renewal, if you enable it, keeps running until you cancel before the next term starts, and a failed automatic payment doesn't extend your service term or wipe the debt.

For anyone whose plan involves running a public proxy, a relay, or automation against a social platform, that list is the deciding document. AkileCloud's plan sheets don't spell out an equivalent list in the material reviewed here, so read its terms yourself if your workload sits near any of those lines.

## Where the money actually differs

**Traffic per dollar.** Take AkileCloud's LAX Lite 1C/1G: $9.99 for 4,000GB works out to $0.0025 per GB. ByteVirt, the name that keeps turning up when people want a China-optimised route instead of raw bandwidth, sells 512MB / 15GB / 500GB on a 500Mbps port at $5.50/month, which is $0.011 per GB. That's 4.4 times AkileCloud's rate on list pricing, or about 3.5 times if you apply the discount ByteVirt advertises (roughly $4.40, or $0.0088 per GB). The Hong Kong NTT entry plan is cheaper still at about $0.0014 per GB, though you get 1TB instead of 4TB and a 1Gbps port instead of 5Gbps.

**What happens after the cap.** AkileCloud throttles to a shared 10Mbps. ByteVirt's CN2 GIA line throttles to 1Mbps. Both keep the server online, and the gap between those two numbers is bigger than most spec sheets suggest: 10Mbps still carries a video stream, 1Mbps carries a terminal session and not much else. This is the most commonly skipped line in budget VPS comparisons and it decides more real-world experience than peak port speed does.

**Backups.** Every ByteVirt CN2 GIA plan includes 3 free snapshots and 1 free backup per server. AkileCloud's published plan tables don't list snapshots or backups as a standard inclusion. Bage's terms place backups squarely on the customer for unmanaged services. If your data matters, price the backup arrangement in before you order, not after.

**Routing.** ByteVirt builds its identity around CN2 GIA return routing from Los Angeles: IPv4 three-network CN2 GIA, IPv6 9929+CMI2, test IP 154.17.30.96. AkileCloud explicitly doesn't guarantee mainland direct connection. Bage makes no routing claim in the documentation reviewed. On this dimension the answer is unambiguous.

**CPU.** None of the three is a compute product. All run fair-share CPU on budget hardware, and reviews of AkileCloud describe CPU and disk I/O as adequate rather than exceptional, which is fine for streaming and forwarding and wrong for compiling or running inference.

## Pick by what you're doing with the box

- **Streaming region-locked content, or forwarding a lot of traffic:** AkileCloud LAX Lite 1C/1G at $9.99. The 5Gbps port and 4TB cap are built for exactly this.
- **Remote desktop, SSH or API calls from mainland China:** ByteVirt CN2 GIA, starting at the 512MB plan. AkileCloud isn't selling that route.
- **The cheapest box that's genuinely usable:** AkileCloud Hong Kong NTT 1C/1G at ¥9.99/month. Accept that the route isn't China-optimised.
- **A specific country's IP for content or platform reasons:** AkileCloud covers Japan, Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, Hong Kong, the US, the UK and Germany; ByteVirt is essentially Los Angeles with a Tokyo option.
- **Public proxy, relay, Tor exit or social-platform automation:** read Bage's acceptable-use policy first. Those are named prohibitions there, and a 24-hour abuse clock runs on your account.
- **Compiling, CI runners, ML inference:** none of the above. You're in the wrong price bracket.

## Three things to confirm at checkout

1. **Whether backups are included.** AkileCloud's plan tables don't list snapshots or backups as standard. Check the panel or ask support before you migrate anything you can't rebuild.
2. **The overage speed you'll actually get.** AkileCloud throttles to a shared 10Mbps once the monthly allowance is gone, and additional traffic can be purchased. Decide whether 10Mbps shared covers your worst week.
3. **Payment method and renewal terms.** Alipay is reported at checkout; other methods vary between reviews (FPS, USDT, credit card, PayPal), so confirm on the payment page. No refund policy turned up in the documentation reviewed for either provider, so read the terms on the checkout screen rather than assuming a cooling-off period. If you enable auto-renewal, cancel before the next term starts, not after.
